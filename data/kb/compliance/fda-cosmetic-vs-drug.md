# FDA: Is it a cosmetic, a drug, or both?

Summary of the FDA page. Quoted text is verbatim; everything else is a paraphrase.

## Legal definitions (FD&C Act)

- **Cosmetic:** articles "intended to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled, or sprayed on ... the human body ... for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering the appearance."
- **Drug:** articles "intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease" and articles "intended to affect the structure or any function of the body."

## Intended use decides the category

FDA looks at three things to decide what a product is intended to do:

1. **Claims** in labeling, advertising, websites and social media. A therapeutic claim makes the product a drug, even if the formula is a normal cosmetic.
2. **Consumer perception**, meaning what the product's reputation leads people to expect.
3. **Ingredients** that are well known for a therapeutic use (for example fluoride in toothpaste).

## Claims that make a product a drug

FDA's examples include claims to:

- "restore hair growth"
- "reduce cellulite"
- "treat varicose veins"
- "increase or decrease the production of melanin (pigment) in the skin"
- "regenerate cells"
- treat dandruff
- protect from the sun (sunscreen claims)
- treat acne

## Products that are both

Some products are both a cosmetic and a drug and must meet both sets of rules. FDA's examples: anti-dandruff shampoos, fluoride toothpastes, antiperspirant deodorants, and **moisturizers or makeup marketed with sun-protection claims**.

## Why this matters for Glow Recipe products

These notes are the knowledge base's own reading of the rule, not FDA text:

- **Watermelon Glow Niacinamide Sunscreen SPF 50 (P481989)** has sun-protection claims, so it is an OTC drug. See brand/claims-policy rule CP-05.
- **Salicylic acid (BHA) products**, such as P482535, P504125 and P458219, are cosmetics only as long as the marketing does not claim to treat acne. See CP-02.
- **"Dark spot" and "brightening" language**, as in Guava Vitamin C Dark Spot Treatment Serum (P475543), must describe appearance ("the look of dark spots"). It must not claim to change melanin production. See CP-03.
