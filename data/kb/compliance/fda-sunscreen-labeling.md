# FDA: OTC sunscreen labeling rules

In the U.S., sunscreens are **over-the-counter (OTC) drugs**. Their labeling is governed by 21 CFR 201.327 (the 2011 labeling and effectiveness testing rule).

## Prohibited terms

The terms **"sunblock"**, **"sweatproof"** and **"waterproof"** are not allowed. Using them, or similar terms, makes the product misbranded under section 502 of the FD&C Act.

## Water resistance

- A product can only claim water resistance if it passes the test in the regulation. The claim must then read **"Water Resistant (40 minutes)"** or **"Water Resistant (80 minutes)"**.
- Products that pass must tell users to reapply "after 40 [or 80] minutes of swimming or sweating", "immediately after towel drying" and "at least every 2 hours".
- Products that do **not** pass must say "reapply at least every 2 hours" and "use a water resistant sunscreen if swimming or sweating".

## Other points

- "Broad Spectrum" may only be claimed if the product passes the broad-spectrum test.
- The SPF value on the label must match test results.

## Relevance

**Watermelon Glow Niacinamide Sunscreen SPF 50 (P481989)** is the only sunscreen in the catalog. The knowledge base does **not** contain its water-resistance or broad-spectrum test results.
