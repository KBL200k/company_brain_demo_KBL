# FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance

Summary of the FTC guidance (December 2022), which replaced the 1998 dietary supplement guide. It covers advertising for health-related products. Skin-health and "clinical" claims for skincare fall under it.

## 1. Every objective claim needs substantiation

Health-related claims must be backed by **"competent and reliable scientific evidence"**: "tests, analyses, research, or studies that (1) have been conducted and evaluated in an objective manner by experts in the relevant disease, condition, or function to which the representation relates; and (2) are generally accepted in the profession to yield accurate and reliable results."

FTC treats randomized controlled human trials as the strongest evidence for health benefit claims.

## 2. Express and implied claims both count

Advertisers must substantiate claims stated directly **and** claims "suggested or reasonably implied" by the ad. What matters is how consumers understand the ad, not what the advertiser meant.

## 3. Net impression

An ad is judged by its **"net impression"**: everything in it together, "including the text, product name, and any charts, graphs, and other images." For example, a product name or a before/after image can create a claim even if the copy never states it.

## 4. Disclosures must be clear and conspicuous

If a claim needs qualifying information, the disclosure must be "easily noticeable" and "easily understandable by ordinary consumers." It must appear in the same format as the claim (on screen for visual claims, spoken for audio claims). Fine print alone is not enough. A disclosure cannot fix a claim that is false.

## 5. Testimonials and endorsements

Advertisers are "liable for the misleading use of endorsements". A customer quote saying a product "cleared my acne" is a claim the brand must be able to substantiate. Material connections with endorsers must be disclosed (see compliance/ftc-endorsement-guides).

## Practical implications (knowledge base's reading)

- "Clinically proven", "dermatologist recommended", "visible results in 7 days" and similar claims need a study on file. The knowledge base contains **no clinical study data** for any product, so the assistant cannot confirm that such a claim is substantiated.
- Quoting a real review in an ad does not exempt the claim it contains. A review quote counts as the brand's own claim. See brand/claims-policy CP-07.
