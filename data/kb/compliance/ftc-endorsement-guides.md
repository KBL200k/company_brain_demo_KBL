# FTC Endorsement Guides (revised 2023)

## When a disclosure is required

An influencer or endorser must disclose a **material connection** to the brand, meaning one "that a significant minority of consumers wouldn't expect and it would affect how they evaluate the endorsement." This includes:

- being paid to mention the product
- **receiving free product**, including PR boxes and gifted items
- an ongoing relationship such as affiliate commissions or ambassador programs. **Each post needs its own disclosure**, even if earlier posts already disclosed it.

## How to disclose

- Simple and direct, for example "Ad", "Paid post" or "#ad", placed at the beginning.
- Hard to miss: visible without clicking "more", not hidden among hashtags, not only in the comments.
- In video, put the disclosure **in the video itself** (on-screen text and spoken), not only in the description.
- Not as a hyperlink.
- **Not adequate:** "#endorsed" (ambiguous), "#gifted" alone (does not say who gave it), or a platform's built-in label alone.

## Truthfulness

Endorsers must:

- only describe experiences they actually had
- not misrepresent how often they use the product
- not make claims the brand could not substantiate
- give their honest opinion, which may include criticism

## Reviews

Companies **must not**:

- ask only happy customers for reviews
- suppress or delay negative reviews while promoting positive ones
- make an incentive conditional on the review being positive
- display reviews in a misleading way (for example always showing 5★ first)

Incentivized reviews need a disclosure on each review. If incentives artificially raise ratings, the practice is still deceptive even with the disclosure.

## Brand responsibilities

Brands should run a reasonable **monitoring program**: train creators on allowed claims and on how to disclose, periodically check what they post, and act on non-compliance. FTC recommends pre-approving posts, especially for health or safety claims.
