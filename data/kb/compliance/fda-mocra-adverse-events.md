# MoCRA: serious adverse event reporting

The Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA) made adverse event reporting mandatory for cosmetics sold in the U.S.

## Reporting duty

- The **responsible person** (manufacturer, packer or distributor named on the label) must report **serious adverse events** linked to a cosmetic product to FDA **within 15 business days** of receiving the report.
- If new medical information arrives **within 1 year** of the first report, it must also be sent to FDA within 15 business days.
- FDA recommends submitting reports on **MedWatch Form 3500A**, either electronically or on paper.

## What counts as "serious"

A serious adverse event includes **significant disfigurement**, for example:

- serious and persistent rashes
- second- or third-degree burns
- significant hair loss
- persistent or significant change in appearance

A reaction also counts as serious if it needs **medical or surgical intervention** to prevent one of these outcomes. Reactions that are expected under normal use do not count.

## Relevance to this knowledge base

Customer reviews sometimes describe burning, rashes or other reactions (see the "Irritation" theme in the research/insights-* docs). A **public review is not by itself a report to the company**, but support tickets and direct messages are. sop/adverse-event-escalation describes how customer-reported reactions are triaged against this definition.
